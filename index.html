The Gap

Instruments for the CRNA

Three small instruments for the time we spend together. A typical shift holds thirty to forty transitions — seams between one thing and the next. These tools are about the seams: finding them, feeling what your body does inside them, and learning to use one on purpose. Open them on your own machine — they run offline and keep everything to themselves.

The high-functioning clinician under pressure is not the calmest one in the room — it's the one whose nervous system has the widest range: quick to mobilize, quick to recover. That range is trainable, in the moments you already have. Durability is a skill, not a temperament.

Instrument One
The NeuroMinute™
Sixty seconds of paced breath, together — an orb sets the tempo near the resonant frequency where heart-rate variability peaks. A one-tap check before and after makes the shift felt, not just described. The live one; we do it as a room.

Instrument Two
The Diaphragmatic Check
Hand on chest, hand on belly — at rest, then under a little mental load. It reveals a blindness that 28 of 30 anesthesia clinicians share: lost conscious contact with the diaphragm. Then a thirty-second re-teach. The signature hook.

Instrument Three
The Gap Moment Audit™
Map your own shift — pre-op, induction, the post-induction seam, emergence, turnover, hand-off, the drive home. Rate each spike, flag two or three to train, and copy a summary to take home. The personal one.
