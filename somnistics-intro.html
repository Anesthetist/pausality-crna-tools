The insight

The training surface was
hiding in plain sight.

A typical CRNA shift holds 30–40 natural transitions — pre-op, induction, the seam before the surgeon starts, emergence, turnover, the drive home. We call them Gap Moments™.

The reframe: a Gap Moment isn't downtime to fill. It's a rep you already own. You don't add a practice to the day — you make conscious a moment that's already there.

The mechanism

Not calmer. Wider range.

The high-functioning clinician under pressure isn't the calmest one in the room — it's the one whose nervous system mobilizes fast and recovers fast. That property is autonomic flexibility, and it is trainable.

Frequency
Resonant breathing
Paced breath near ~5.5/min drives heart-rate variability to its peak — a measurable autonomic shift within a single session.
Skill
Diaphragmatic literacy
Feeling and steering the diaphragm on purpose — the prerequisite most experienced clinicians have quietly lost.
Outcome
Autonomic flexibility
A wider envelope of mobilize-and-recover. Training doesn't lower arousal; it enlarges the range.
The signature finding

Most clinicians have lost contact
with the muscle they manage all day.

In an informal test of 30 anesthesia professionals on their breaks, we asked them to engage the diaphragm on command. How many could?

2 of 30

A singer and a trumpet player — the two who had built the cortical map through art. The other 28 breathed with accessory muscles alone. With 30 seconds of coaching, all 30 reached a 10-second diaphragmatic inhale. The capacity was always there. The awareness wasn't — and awareness is trainable.
